When someone searches for "alarm company near me" or "security guard services" plus a city name, Google usually shows a map with a short list of businesses before any regular website results. That list comes from Google Business Profile. For many security companies, it is the first place a buyer sees them — and sometimes the only place, because a buyer can call straight from the listing without ever visiting a website.

The good news is that most of what makes a profile work is within your control. This guide walks through the parts that matter most for security companies, in the order we would tackle them.

## Start with the basics: verification and accurate details

Before anything else, make sure you own and control your profile. If a former employee or an old agency set it up, request access so the account belongs to the company, not to one person's personal Google account.

Then check every detail for accuracy:

- **Business name.** Use your real business name exactly as it appears on your signage and paperwork. Adding keywords or city names to the name ("Acme Security – Best Alarm Company Dallas") breaks Google's guidelines and can get a profile suspended.
- **Phone number.** Use a number that is answered during your listed hours. A local number is fine; so is a tracking number, as long as your main number is listed as an additional phone.
- **Website link.** Link to the page most relevant to local buyers — often your home page, or a location page if you have several offices.
- **Hours.** Be honest. If you offer 24/7 monitoring but your sales office is open 8 to 5, list the hours when someone can actually respond to a new inquiry, and mention round-the-clock monitoring in your description.

## Choose categories carefully

Your primary category has a large influence on which searches your profile can appear for. Pick the one that describes the core of your business, then add secondary categories for the other services you genuinely provide.

For security companies, that often means choosing between options such as "Security guard service", "Security system supplier" and "Security system installer". A guard company and an alarm installer should not share a primary category, even if their work occasionally overlaps. It is also worth checking which categories the companies in your map results use, to make sure you have not missed one that describes work you already do.

## Set your service area properly

Many security companies do not have a storefront that customers visit. If that is you, Google lets you hide your address and list the areas you serve instead. This is the correct setup for a service-area business, and it keeps a home office address private.

Two points to keep in mind:

1. List the cities, counties or regions you actually serve. Listing an enormous area does not make you appear everywhere; the map results still lean heavily toward businesses close to the searcher.
2. If you have real offices in more than one city — staffed locations, not virtual offices or mailboxes — each can have its own verified profile. Creating profiles at addresses where you have no real presence breaks Google's rules and puts all of your listings at risk.

## Fill in services, description and attributes

Buyers compare several listings side by side, so give them enough information to choose you.

- **Services.** Add each service you sell: guard services, mobile patrol, alarm installation, monitoring, video surveillance, access control, and so on.
- **Description.** Explain who you serve, the areas you cover and your licensing. Write for people, not for search engines.

## Keep the profile active with posts and photos

A profile that was last updated years ago looks like a business that may no longer be operating. Regular activity shows buyers you are open and paying attention.

**Photos** do the most work. Good options for security companies include:

- Your team in uniform, with permission
- Branded vehicles on patrol or at a job
- Clean, finished installations: cameras, control panels, access readers
- Your office or monitoring center, if you have one

Avoid stock images, which tell a buyer nothing about your company, and avoid photos that reveal a client's security layout in a recognizable building.

**Posts** are short updates on your profile. Use them for seasonal reminders, new services, recent project types or hiring announcements. A steady rhythm matters more than any single post.

## Build a steady flow of genuine reviews

Reviews influence both where you appear and whether a buyer calls. The key is a simple, consistent process rather than occasional pushes.

1. **Ask at the right moment.** For installers, that is usually right after a clean install and a walkthrough. For guard companies, it may be after a smooth first month on a new site or a well-handled event.
2. **Make it easy.** Send the direct review link from your profile by text or email, so the customer lands on the review form in one tap.
3. **Ask everyone the same way.** Do not ask only the customers you think are happiest. Google's guidelines prohibit selectively soliciting positive reviews.
4. **Never buy, write or incentivize reviews.** Offering discounts or gifts in exchange for reviews breaks Google's rules, and fake reviews can lead to removed reviews or a suspended profile.

Reply to every review. Thank people briefly for positive ones. For negative ones, respond calmly, acknowledge the concern and offer to continue the conversation offline — future buyers read your replies to see how you handle problems.

## Watch for unwanted edits and spam listings

Anyone can suggest edits to a Business Profile, and Google sometimes applies them without asking the owner. Check your profile regularly for changed hours, phone numbers, categories or websites. If you spot a listing in your map results that clearly breaks Google's guidelines, such as a fake business at an invented address, you can report it through Google's tools.

## Connect the profile to your website and follow-up

A profile's job is to start a conversation. Make sure the next steps work:

- The website link goes to a fast page that makes it easy to request a quote or site survey.
- Calls from the profile are answered, or returned quickly.
- You can tell which inquiries came from your profile, using the calls and website clicks in its performance view plus call or form tracking on your site.

Your profile and your website support each other. The profile drives the map results, while your website drives the regular results beneath them and gives buyers the detail they need before they commit. ## A simple monthly routine

If you do nothing else, set aside time once a month to:

- Check that your details, hours and categories are still correct
- Add a few new photos and at least one post
- Reply to any reviews you have not answered
- Look at the performance view for calls and website clicks
- Scan the map results for spam listings

None of this is complicated, but it does need to happen consistently — and that consistency is what separates a profile that produces calls from one that sits untouched.
